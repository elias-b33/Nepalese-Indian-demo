The kitchen

Every Nepali home has a chulo at its centre.

It's the clay hearth where the rice is cooked, the tea is boiled twice and everyone ends up sitting, whether they were invited into the kitchen or not. Ours is a little bigger and burns oak, but the idea hasn't changed.

Sunita grew up in Bhaktapur folding momo for her father's tea shop. Rajan's family cooked for weddings in Lucknow. They met on the line of a London kitchen in 2009, argued about dal for five years, and opened Chulo to settle it. The menu is what happened when neither of them won.
